# Learning evidence in the catalog

Reviewed September 15, 2026 UTC. Portal dataset **350**, [Bennerstedt and Sjöblom (2026), Educational Wargaming in Higher Education](https://sjms.nu/articles/10.31374/sjms.451), is used as an educational design reference. Its review of fifteen studies uses the Presage–Process–Product model and identifies weaknesses in assessment, debriefing research and longitudinal evidence. This motivates the following REPLAY design choices; the article does not validate REPLAY.

| Evaluation layer | REPLAY evidence | What still needs a human |
|---|---|---|
| Conditions | Scenario version, role, initial state, permitted information, assistance and source provenance | Instructor confirms the task tests the intended competency |
| Process | Decisions, evidence checks, delegation, source updates and review annotations | Learner explains choices; instructor interprets cited evidence |
| Outcomes | Recorded result, rubric observations, branch practice and separate later probes | Assess transfer on a new task; do not infer learning from winning |

Authored cases illustrate the workflow. Model games test software and opponent behavior. Neither is a substitute for expert learner evidence.
